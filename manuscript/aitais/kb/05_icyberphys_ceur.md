## 4. CEUR-WS.org policies

Main source: https://ceur-ws.org/HOWTOSUBMIT.html (title "valid from 2019-08-01"; footer "21-Apr-1995 (23-Jun-2026/MJ)";
server Last-Modified 2026-10-16). Re-verified 2026-10-22.

### 4.1 Grounds for declining a volume

- "CEUR-WS.org is not obliged to publish a submission"; "The CEUR-WS Team is not obliged to justify its decision."
- **Peer review.** "Proceedings papers must be peer-reviewed by a program committee of well-known international scholars."
  Acceptance must be "purely based on the quality and relevance of papers". The PC and the selection rules must appear in
  the preface. No minimum number of reviewers is set.
- **Size.** "at least six papers in a submitted volume" and "at least 40 pages excluding frontmatter".
- **Event format.** "The workshop must take place (physically) at one location" and "We do not publish proceedings for
  virtual and on-line events" (6th precondition, "Single location, single time"). Hybrid or individual remote talks are not
  addressed (section 6.1).
- **Scope and topics.** The main topic must be computer science, and CEUR may ask for DBLP footprints. CEUR "shall very
  likely reject proceedings submissions that have a very broad set of topics". Student-only proceedings are not supported.
  Most papers must be in English.
- **Diversity.** No institution may contribute more than 33 % of the papers (first author or co-author). No organizing-
  committee member may have more than 1 paper. No institution may supply more than 25 % of the PC.
- **Paper length.**
  - 1 standard page = 2500 characters; minimum for any regular or short paper: five standard pages.
  - Regular paper: "at least 10 'standard' pages". Short paper: 5-9 standard pages.
  - The ICyberPhyS paper has about 31.9 k characters (about 12.8 standard pages), so it qualifies as a regular paper.
- **GenAI.** The GenAI policy page (not HOWTOSUBMIT) says a violation "will lead to the removal of the published paper or the
  whole volume, similar to our procedures dealing with plagiarism" (https://ceur-ws.org/GenAI/Policy.html).
- **DOIs (new).** FAQ 18: after a test phase in Q4 2026, DOIs will be assigned to papers in volumes published from January
  2027; the home page (2026-10-19) says CEUR is experimenting with DOIs for some volumes.

### 4.2 Re-submitting a paper from a declined volume

- **No explicit rule.** HOWTOSUBMIT does not cover authors whose volume was declined.
- **FAQ 19 does not apply.** It covers papers rejected by a main-track programme committee: "CEUR-WS has a policy of not
  publishing rejected papers." Paper 9 was accepted by the PC; only the volume was declined.
- **The test is originality.** An unpublished paper can go to another CEUR volume if it passes the originality rule (4.3).
  The obstacle is the MAKE article, not ICyberPhyS. Under decisions 12-14 the AITAIS paper is a new paper (AI4I data, new
  numbers), not a re-submission of ICyberPhyS.

### 4.3 Prior publication and prior presentation

- **Originality rule:** "The papers must be original, i.e. not published in an earlier workshop or conference or journal!"
- **No double publication.** "The same paper should not be published twice, unless there are special circumstances", and
  "do not publish papers with us that are already published by another publisher". Re-publication "could be regarded as
  self-plagiarism or double publication."
- **Extended versions (FAQ 3).** FAQ 3 concerns a CEUR paper later extended for a journal or major conference: some
  communities accept this "as long as the extended version significantly exceeds the original paper and a reference to the
  original paper is given"; CEUR has "no role in judging particular cases". It does not cover the reverse direction (journal
  first, workshop later), so it is only an analogy for AITAIS.
- **Pre-published papers.** The HTML source shows that the old "pre-published paper" category (abstract of a paper already
  published elsewhere) was deprecated on 2026-01-30 "because it potentially can be regarded as a duplicate publication".
- **Prior presentation.** Presentation without publication is not addressed, so it is not a CEUR obstacle.
- **Preprints.** Self-archiving is "not regarded as a re-publication", and authors may publish "a pre-print, post-print or the
  published version" of their CEUR paper. Nothing says an earlier preprint blocks publication.

### 4.5 Generative AI policy

Sources: https://ceur-ws.org/GenAI/Policy.html and https://ceur-ws.org/GenAI/Taxonomy.html (both "Published on 18-Dec-2024",
by Salatino, Fornari and Zdravkovic; server Last-Modified 2025-12-04). The template's link ceur-ws.org/genai-tax.html
redirects to the taxonomy page. Re-verified 2026-10-22.

- **Dates.** The policy took effect 2025-01-01 (replacing CEUR's 2023 policy). The declaration became mandatory on 2025-06-01.
- **What authors must do.**
  - Declare GenAI use in a dedicated section, "Declaration on Generative AI", which appears above the References.
  - List every GenAI tool and service, and describe each tool's contribution "categorised in accordance with the established
    GenAI Usage Taxonomy".
  - Review and edit any AI-generated content; authors are accountable. AI cannot be listed as an author.
- **GenAI Usage Taxonomy: exact current terms** (13 "GenAI Contribution Roles", in page order):
  1. Drafting content
  2. Generate images
  3. Text Translation
  4. Generate literature review
  5. Paraphrase and reword
  6. Improve writing style
  7. Abstract drafting
  8. Grammar and spelling check
  9. Plagiarism detection
  10. Citation management
  11. Formatting assistance
  12. Peer review simulation
  13. Content enhancement
- **Listing a term does not make the use acceptable.** The taxonomy is a vocabulary for declaring use, not a list of permitted
  uses. The policy table says "Using GenAI to generate new text, such as paragraphs, or even entire sections of a paper is
  ethically unacceptable", and "Employing GenAI to create visual aids, such as diagrams, charts, and illustrations is
  unacceptable" (image generation is acceptable only when the paper's core topic is image generation). HOWTOSUBMIT FAQ 16:
  "Generating text paragraphs or even whole sections by AI is in general not permitted." So "Drafting content", "Generate
  literature review", "Abstract drafting", "Generate images" and "Content enhancement" are high-risk declarations for AITAIS.
- **No term for code or analysis (inferred).** The taxonomy covers writing tasks only. Claude's planned roles (implementing
  the AI4I experiment, producing tables and plots, alt-text drafts, section reviews; decisions 23, 25) have no exact term.
  "Peer review simulation" fits section reviews; "Grammar and spelling check", "Improve writing style" and "Paraphrase and
  reword" fit language edits. Data plots drawn by code from real results are not "generated images" in the policy's sense
  (inferred), but the F1 pipeline diagram should be drawn or substantively edited by the authors, since AI-created diagrams
  are named as unacceptable. Describe AI-written analysis code in plain words in the same declaration (inferred).
- **Template wording** (local AITAIS template `CEUR-Template-1col-1.odt`, matching CEUR's):
  - Option 1: "The author(s) have not employed any Generative AI tools."
  - Option 2 ("by using the activity taxonomy in ceur-ws.org/genai-tax.html"): "During the preparation of this work, the
    author(s) used X in order to: [taxonomy terms]. After using these tool(s)/service(s), the author(s) reviewed and edited
    the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the publication's content."
- **ICyberPhyS version.** It declared Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (Google LLC) and Grammarly for "copyediting assistance" in free
  wording, without taxonomy terms. For AITAIS, list the tools actually used (decision 7) with taxonomy terms.

## 6. Presentation and deadline

### 6.1 Online talk at an offline event

- **Facts.** The talk will be given online by Pavlo Radiuk alone, as agreed with the AIT&AIS'2026 organising committee,
  because travel is unsafe due to the war (missile and drone attacks). AIT&AIS'2026 is announced as an offline (in-person)

- **CEUR rule.** The "Single location, single time" precondition concerns the **event**: it must take place physically at one
  location, and CEUR does not publish "virtual and on-line events". HOWTOSUBMIT says nothing about individual remote talks.
- **Evidence.** CEUR publishes events with remote participation when they have a physical location: AIT&AIS 2025 was hybrid
  (`03_aitais_venue.md`) and is CEUR Vol-4160, listed as "Chernivtsi, Ukraine, December 18-19, 2025"; DETECH 2026 is listed
  as "Hybrid Event, Zadar, Croatia" (Vol-4215).
- **Assessment (inferred):** one online talk at an offline event creates **no material CEUR risk**; the volume would still
  describe a physical event in Chernivtsi. The volume-level risk exists only if the event as a whole becomes mostly or fully
  online (outside author control). The real dependency is AIT&AIS's own rule that unpresented papers are not published
